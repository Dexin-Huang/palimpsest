## MEDIEVAL LATIN PALEOGRAPHY

### Script Recognition

Medieval Latin manuscripts use various hands. Common features:

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Gothic textura | Angular, compressed letterforms |
| Gothic cursiva | More flowing, connected strokes |
| Humanist | Rounder, classical revival (15th c.) |

### Easily Confused Letters

| Confused pair | How to distinguish |
|---------------|-------------------|
| c vs t | t has crossbar extending right |
| c vs e | e has horizontal tongue |
| u vs n | identical in many hands - use context |
| i vs l vs long i | l taller; long i (j) descends |
| f vs long s (ſ) | f has crossbar on both sides; ſ only left or none |
| r vs 2-shaped r | 2-shaped r after round letters (o, p, b, d) |
| d vs cl | uncial d is round; cl has gap |

## DECORATED INITIALS

**CRITICAL:** Decorated initials are large ornate letters (2-8 lines tall) that BEGIN sections. They are SINGLE LETTERS, not numbers or symbols.

Common misreadings to AVOID:
| Misread as | Actually is |
|------------|-------------|
| "B3" or "13" | Ornate "D" (De...) |
| "JN" or "IN" together | Ornate "I" followed by normal "n" |
| "O" with marks | Ornate "Q" (Quod..., Quia...) |
| Complex shape | Ornate "S", "P", "A", "C" |
| Two letters | Single letter with pen flourishes |

**How to identify:**
- Large element at paragraph/section start
- May contain pen flourishes, faces, animals, or geometric patterns
- Often in blue, red, or gold (or spaces left for rubrication)
- Text continues with lowercase immediately after
- Latin sections commonly start: "De...", "In...", "Quod...", "Si...", "Item...", "Cum..."

## RUBRICS (RED TEXT)

Rubrics are headers, titles, or highlighting in red ink:
- Section titles: "De calcinatione mercurii"
- Chapter markers: "Capitulum primum"
- Important terms highlighted mid-text
- Instructions to reader: "Nota bene", "Sequitur"

Always mark rubrics with `"type": "rubric"` and `"style": {"color": "rubric"}`.

## TWO-COLUMN LAYOUT

If the page has two columns:

**How to identify:**
- Text on left and right with clear gap/ruling between
- Lines do NOT continue across - columns are separate
- Reading order: left column top-to-bottom, THEN right column top-to-bottom

**Zone ordering:**
- Left column zones: order 1, 2, 3...
- Right column zones: order continues after left column ends
- Marginalia: order after main text, or use high numbers (900+)

**IMPORTANT:** Do NOT interleave columns. Transcribe each column completely before moving to the next.

## MARGINALIA

Marginal annotations may include:
- **Glosses:** Explanations of difficult terms
- **Corrections:** Scribal or later corrections
- **Cross-references:** "vide supra", "ut infra"
- **Reader notes:** Later scholarly additions (if medieval, include; if modern, exclude)
- **Manicula:** Pointing hands (☞) marking important passages

Position marginalia zones using bbox_norm relative to the main text block.

- **Apparatus drawings:** Furnaces, alembics, retorts, crucibles

- **0.95-1.00:** Clear, unambiguous reading
