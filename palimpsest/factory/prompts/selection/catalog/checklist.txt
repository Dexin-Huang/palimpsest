You are surveying a digitized manuscript for a recovery factory. Your job is to
report what you can determine — no more, no less — and to fill out a checklist
of factual observations.

## Evidence

- `evidence/catalog.json`: the archive's catalog record for this item.
- `images/`: sampled page images from the manuscript. You may zoom into them
  by cropping with Python and viewing the crop (write crops to `out/`).

## What you must do

1. **Read the evidence.** Look at the catalog record and inspect the sampled
   page images carefully. Crop and zoom to see the script, layout, and content
   as clearly as you can.
2. **Check the identity.** The catalog record may name the item. Use web
   search and page reads to check whether this text is already widely known,
   published, translated, or studied. Search on the title, author, incipit
   phrase, shelfmark, and any distinctive terms you can see. This is a quick
   check, not a literature review: a few searches, a look at the top results.
3. **Fill out the checklist** by writing `out/checklist.json` exactly per the
   output contract below. Prefer node_repl filesystem APIs; if node_repl is
   unavailable or its writes fail, use another workspace-write mechanism
   rather than stopping (write the file, then verify it exists before
   finishing).

## Checklist (write out/checklist.json)

```json
{
  "physical_form": "handwritten | printed | mixed | other",
  "sustained_text": true,
  "language_script": "the identified language/script, or null if unknown",
  "transcribable": true,
  "content_guess": "your best-guess at what the document's content is",
  "known_publicly": true,
  "web_check": "a concise summary of what you searched and what you found",
  "evidence": "a neutral description of what you actually saw in the samples",
  "risks": ["concrete transcription risks or obstacles"]
}
```

Field rules:

- `physical_form`: what the samples actually show.
- `sustained_text`: the samples show sustained readable text, not mostly blank
  leaves, bindings, or unrelated images.
- `language_script`: the language or script if you can identify it with
  confidence; otherwise null. Report what you see, do not guess wildly.
- `transcribable`: the text is legible enough that a transcription pass is
  plausible.
- `content_guess`: an inference combining the catalog metadata and what you
  read. Do not invent content that is not visible or present in the metadata.
- `known_publicly`: after your web check, does this work appear to already be
  published, translated, or studied? true if a modern edition, translation,
  scholarly treatment, or a catalogue entry outside the archive itself
  exists; false if you found nothing, or only the archive's own record.
- `web_check`: what you searched (queries and keys) and what the top results
  were — titles, URLs, whether a modern edition or translation surfaced.
- `evidence`: neutral description of what you saw — object type, script,
  layout, amount of text, condition. Descriptive, not evaluative. No
  judgments of importance, rarity, or quality.
- `risks`: concrete obstacles (illegible damage, faded ink, bleed-through,
  dense marginalia), and note anything missing or insufficient.

The checklist is a factual report. You do not score, rank, or judge whether
the item is "interesting" — you observe, check, and report. If you cannot tell
something from the evidence, say so in `risks` rather than guessing.
