# Borg.cin.361 document dossier

Date: 2026-03-08

Status: working manuscript-level understanding, not a final scholarly identification

## Current best description

`Borg.cin.361` is not a single narrow text. It appears to be a multilingual
Jesuit-era working compilation centered on Chinese classical thought, especially
Yijing and Neo-Confucian cosmology, with substantial French interpretive and
polemical commentary layered over Chinese source material.

The manuscript matters because it preserves not only Chinese text, but a mode of
cross-cultural reading:

- Chinese source passages
- French explanatory notes
- Romanized Chinese names and terms
- diagram pages
- mission-era apologetic or polemical argument

That makes it more than a copy of a Chinese classic. It looks like a record of
how a learned European reader, likely in a missionary context, actively worked
through Chinese texts.

## Strongest evidence so far

### 1. Bilingual metaphysical section

The section around `f200r-f202r` is the clearest philosophical core recovered so
far.

Artifacts:

- `experiments/f200r_witness_v1/f200r_reading.md`
- `experiments/f200r_f202r_synthesis_v2/section_synthesis.md`

What it shows:

- explicit treatment of `五行`, `陰陽`, `太極`, and `無極`
- references to Zhou Dunyi, the Cheng brothers, Zhu Xi, Xu Shen, `Shuowen`,
  and `Zhuzi Quanshu`
- French argument about whether Chinese cosmology is atheistic
- explicit comparison to Descartes and Spinozism

Working interpretation:

- this section is trying to make Neo-Confucian metaphysics legible and defensible
  within a European conceptual frame
- the manuscript is not merely descriptive; it is argumentative

### 2. Pure Chinese classical-philosophical section

The section around `f150r-f152r` is a pure Chinese prose cluster focused on
ritual and moral order.

Artifacts:

- `experiments/f150r_witness_v3/f150r_reading.md`
- `experiments/f150r_f152r_synthesis_v1/section_synthesis.md`

What it shows:

- metaphors of plumb line, balance, and compass/square as objective standards
- distinction between those without direction and those governed by ritual
- movement toward sagehood through discipline and love of ritual

Working interpretation:

- this section aligns strongly with `Xunzi` / `Li lun` style discourse
- the manuscript therefore ranges beyond cosmology into moral-philosophical and
  canonical material

## Best current thesis

The manuscript is best understood not as an "unknown Chinese text" but as an
underdescribed multilingual intellectual compilation.

Current best thesis:

- a Jesuit-era or mission-era working manuscript
- built around Chinese cosmology, Yijing, Neo-Confucian lineage, and ritual
- containing both witness excerpts and European interpretive overlays
- possibly assembled for teaching, translation, apologetics, or doctrinal
  comparison

What makes it high-value:

- it preserves a cross-cultural reading practice, not just source content
- it may show how Chinese metaphysics was translated into European categories in
  working notes rather than polished publication
- the coexistence of diagrams, bilingual argument, classical Chinese prose, and
  missionary language makes it unusually rich
