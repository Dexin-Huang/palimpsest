## What It Is

This is not a single narrow `Yijing` commentary. It is a practical divination and fate-calculation handbook.

The surviving object appears to combine at least four layers:

1. Named divination entries with culture-hero or historical labels:
   - `Zhou Gong gua`
   - `Kongzi gua`
   - `Qu Yuan gua`
   - `Jie Zhou gua`
   - `Yue Wang gua`

2. More standard hexagram-based divination:
   - `Gu`
   - `Xun`
   - `Zhen`
   - `Gen`
   - other gua entries with explicit auspicious / inauspicious outcomes

3. Zodiac / branch-based fate profiles:
   - birth sign
   - associated directional emperor or star
   - prescribed grain, clothing color, and medicine color
   - major and minor calamity years
   - taboo months and social restrictions
   - lifespan and status predictions

4. Bodily omen interpretation:
   - ear ringing
   - ear heat
   - face heat
   - eye twitching
   - heart agitation
   - itching of feet or palms
   - keyed to time of day and event prediction

## Strongest Findings

### Pages 0-1

The opening is a practical rod-divination section. It explicitly describes a method using rods and then gives named divination entries with direct outcomes.

Examples:
- `Zhou Gong gua`: sickness resolves, disaster departs, blessings arrive, wealth enters, traveler returns safely, `greatly auspicious`
- `Kongzi gua`: anxiety first, prosperity later
- `Qu Yuan gua`: official delay, illness difficulty, unsettled residence, `greatly inauspicious`
- `Jie Zhou gua`: blocked road, loss, unreasonable official affairs, `greatly inauspicious`

This makes the manuscript feel operational, not literary.

### Pages 3-6

The manuscript broadens into a mixed divination / hemerological system:
- named gua continue
- standard `Yijing` hexagrams appear
- advice covers official affairs, travel, marriage, recovery from illness, and profit
- by pages 4-6 the handbook clearly turns to zodiac-born fate rules keyed to color, medicine, and taboo years

Examples:
- people born under different signs are assigned to directional emperors
- specific grains and clothing colors are prescribed
- black, red, yellow, white, or green medicines are recommended depending on the sign cluster
- certain years or months are marked as major or minor calamity points
- visiting the sick or attending funerals is forbidden in certain configurations

### Pages 7-11

The later middle section becomes more biographical and karmic in tone.

It includes:
- past-life or former-origin style statements
- predictions of office, wealth, spouse, children, and servants
- lifespan estimates
- continued taboo geography and conduct rules

The sampled pages suggest a syncretic mode: practical fate calculation mixed with moral or karmic framing.

### Pages 9-10

This section is especially vivid.

It reads bodily sensations as omens:
- right ear ringing means wealth or news
- foot itching signals arrival of guests
- face heat, eye twitching, and heart movement signal speech, women, travel, or other incoming events

This is exactly the kind of lived-world trace Palimpsest is looking for: a manual for interpreting ordinary bodily experience as meaningful.

## Why It Matters

This is a much better north-star witness than a lexicon or abstract philosophical treatise.

It preserves a practical world in which:
- historical figures become divinatory archetypes
- bodily sensations are read as signs
- colors, grains, stars, emperors, and directions all structure fate
- medicine and taboo behavior are part of one predictive system

That is exactly the sort of vanished everyday cosmology that modern catalog descriptions flatten away.
