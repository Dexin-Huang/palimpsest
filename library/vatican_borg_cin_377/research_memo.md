## Working Characterization

`Borg.cin.377` is not a single tidy text. It is a working Jesuit dossier or
miscellany: recipes, distribution lists, expense accounts, correspondence
extracts, mission politics, travel notes, astronomy work for the emperor, and
book-shipment catalogs.

The manuscript already looks like a strong match for the Palimpsest north star:
it preserves operational detail about how knowledge, money, books, and people
moved between Beijing, Rome, and Europe.

## Strong Findings

### 1. Mixed practical notebook, not a pure theological treatise

On [f008r_diplomatic.txt](D:/Projects/palimpsest/library/vatican_borg_cin_377/exports/restoration/pages/f008r_diplomatic.txt) the same page contains:
- a French lyric fragment
- Italian notes on gold and silver valuation
- a medal-grading scheme for the Abbe de Rothelin
- Chinese recipe material

The Chinese recipe section appears to include:
- a papaya paste or jam preparation
- an olive-kernel or nut cake preparation

This is a real miscellany page, not a copied literary work.

### 2. Direct distribution network for Chinese chronology sheets

[f008v_diplomatic.txt](D:/Projects/palimpsest/library/vatican_borg_cin_377/exports/restoration/pages/f008v_diplomatic.txt) records the sale and distribution of `Chronolog. Chin.` sheets and then a larger distribution of `150 feuilles chronologiques`.

Named recipients include:
- `m. le President de Montesquieu`
- `au Roy d'Angleterre`
- `au Pape`
- `C. Passionei`
- `card. Fleuri`
- `l'abbe Bignon`
- `bibliotheque de la Propagande`

This is one of the strongest pages in the manuscript. It documents an actual
circulation network for Chinese chronological material into elite European
scholarly and political circles.

### 3. Everyday expense ledger from Beijing / Peking

[f015r_diplomatic.txt](D:/Projects/palimpsest/library/vatican_borg_cin_377/exports/restoration/pages/f015r_diplomatic.txt) is a mundane but valuable household ledger.

It includes dated provisions and costs such as:
- rice
- fish
- chicken
- eggs
- vinegar
- pine wood for fuel
- wine
- sardines
- porcelain

The page explicitly marks `Janvier 1708` and contains `nourriture depuis le 26 novembre que depar de Pekiny`.

This is useful because it anchors the manuscript in lived material history, not
just abstract controversy.

### 4. Detailed account of papal handling of the Chinese rites controversy

[f033v_diplomatic.txt](D:/Projects/palimpsest/library/vatican_borg_cin_377/exports/restoration/pages/f033v_diplomatic.txt) preserves a detailed French-Latin account of a papal audience dated `2. jour d'octobre (1719)`.

The page includes:
- the Pope receiving the Jesuit superior and assistants
- mention of `un long silence qui dura une bonne heure et demie`
- insistence on `parfaite obeissance`
- discussion of the 1704 decree
- remarks on Cardinal de Tournon
- discussion of what could or could not be permitted to Chinese Christians regarding rites for the dead
- mention of the Bishop of Peking

Even if derivative rather than autograph, this is the kind of operational
political reporting that scholars care about because it preserves sequence,
tone, and internal framing.

### 5. First-person astronomy work for the emperor

[f057r_diplomatic.txt](D:/Projects/palimpsest/library/vatican_borg_cin_377/exports/restoration/pages/f057r_diplomatic.txt) is one of the most important folios recovered so far.

It includes first-person statements:
- `J'arrivai a Peking appelle' par l'empereur le 7. aout.`
- delivery of silver / taels after arrival
- `A Peking le 9 Fevr. envoye ... un petit demi cercle de cuivre gradue`
- in `1715`, the writer says he personally gave the emperor the `second volume sur les eclipses avec la machine de la lune et 3 grandes figures`
- the emperor had previously received the first volume
- the writer explains the lunar machine over several days so a `Jeune mathematicien` could explain it to the regent
- bronze machines on planets and eclipses are mentioned
- in `1717`, the writer says he gave a volume on the fixed stars to the emperor and explained it himself

This is not generic "Jesuits did astronomy in China." It is page-level
operational testimony.

### 6. Continued astronomy and binding / production record

[f057v_diplomatic.txt](D:/Projects/palimpsest/library/vatican_borg_cin_377/exports/restoration/pages/f057v_diplomatic.txt) continues the same lane:

That makes the manuscript useful not only for intellectual history, but also

### 7. Book-shipment catalog from Father Fouquet

[f119v_diplomatic.txt](D:/Projects/palimpsest/library/vatican_borg_cin_377/exports/restoration/pages/f119v_diplomatic.txt) lists books sent by `le P. Fouquet` on ships of the East India Company in `1720`.

The catalog includes:

- `Tables mathematiques`
- `Memoires de mathematique`
- `Traite de l'aimant`
- `Observations physiques`
- `Traite d'Algebre`
- `Analyse de l'infinitement petit`
- `Elemens de geometrie`
- `Pensees de Pascal`
- `Moliere`
- `Montfaucon`
- `Nouveau Testament`
- `Reflexions sur les missions`

This is excellent evidence for the actual intellectual cargo moving through the
missionary network.

That is much more interesting than a normal copy-text manuscript. It looks like

## Why It Matters

This manuscript is valuable because it records systems, not just ideas:
- who got what
- what was shipped
- what was paid
- what was presented to the emperor
- how papal decisions were described inside the mission

That is exactly the sort of under-read historical evidence that becomes much
more usable once transcribed and indexed.
