## CORRECTED IDENTIFICATION

**Catalog said**: "Rappresentazioni allegoriche, riproduzioni (?) di arazzi (?)" / "Triumph of Death"

**Actually is**: Complete expanded Trionfi cycle with at least 6 distinct triumphs, exceeding Petrarch's canonical six

## TRIUMPH SEQUENCE

### 1. Triumph of Fame/Mars (ff. 1v-2v)
- **Chariot pulled by**: Lions
- **Central figure**: Mars (Martis) - armored, with flames
- **Attendants**: Fama (Fame with trumpet), Audacia (Boldness), Ambitio (Ambition)
- **Following procession**: Famous warriors

### 2. Procession of Warriors (ff. 2r-3r)
Nine Worthies tradition EXPANDED to include:
- **Classical**: Hector, Alexander Magnus, Julius Caesar, Scipio, Hannibal
- **Biblical/Ancient**: Semiramis, Penthesilea, Cyrus
- **Medieval/Eastern**: Zenobia, Tamerlane
- **Controversial**: "Mahometes" (Muhammad) - significant iconographic choice

### 3. Triumph of Avarice/Plutus (ff. 3v-4v)
- **Chariot pulled by**: Wolves
- **Central figure**: Plutus (god of wealth)
- **Attendants**: Harpya (Harpy), Usura (Usury), Avaricia (Greed)
- **Moralizing label**: Vana Laetitia (Vain Joy)
- **NOT in standard Petrarchan cycle**

### 4. Triumph of Learning/Wisdom (ff. 5v-6r)
- **Procession of scholars**:
  - Philosophers: Aristotle, Pythagoras, Plato (?)
  - Scientists: Archimedes, Ptolemy, Euclid
  - Writers: Cicero, Orpheus
  - Physicians: Dioscorides, Hippocrates (Apocras)
  - Jurists: Papinianus
- **Personification**: Memoria (Memory)
- **NOT in standard Petrarchan cycle**

### 5. Triumph of Libido/Lust (ff. 7v-8r)
- **Chariot pulled by**: Four Pegasi (winged horses)
- **Central figure**: Libido (semi-nude figure)
- **Attendants**:
  - Ovid and Tibullus (Roman erotic poets)
  - Satyrs with pipes
  - Bacchantes
  - Musicians
- **Classical setting**: Bridge, columns, statuary
- **NOT in standard Petrarchan cycle** - highly unusual subject

### 6. Triumph of Death (ff. 9v-10r)
- **Chariot figure**: Mortis (winged Death)
- **Trampled beneath**: Jupiter, Mars, Titus, "Merennius papa" (Pope)
- **Demonic attendant**: Horned figure with scythe
- **Right side**: Mouth of Hell (Infernus) consuming figures
- **Standard Petrarchan subject but with unusual figures**

## ICONOGRAPHIC SIGNIFICANCE

1. **Expanded cycle**: More triumphs than Petrarch's six (Love, Chastity, Death, Fame, Time, Eternity)
2. **Unusual subjects**: Avarice, Learning, Libido - rare in Trionfi tradition
3. **Named figures**: Systematic labeling allows precise identification
4. **Cultural mixing**: Classical, Biblical, Medieval, Islamic figures together
5. **Muhammad included**: Controversial iconographic choice for 16th century
6. **Moralizing tone**: Labels like "Vain Joy", "Poverty" add didactic element
