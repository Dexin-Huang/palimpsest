Significance

This drawing is part of a Trionfi cycle, likely inspired by Petrarch's *Trionfi*. The presence of Plutus (god of wealth), Usura (Usury), Anaitis (an ancient goddess associated with fertility and abundance), and Vana Luxuria (vain luxury) suggests that this triumph is related to Avarice or Wealth. The inclusion of a Harpy, a creature associated with greed and torment, further reinforces this interpretation. The figure labeled "Agricultural Pharos" is unusual and requires further investigation. The overall composition and style are consistent with 16th-century Italian manuscript illumination. The combination of figures suggests a complex allegorical meaning related to the dangers and temptations of wealth.
